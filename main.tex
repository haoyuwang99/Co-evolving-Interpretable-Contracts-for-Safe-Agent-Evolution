\begin{abstract}

Self-evolving agents can acquire effects that their executable safety contracts do not constrain. We propose policy-guided contract co-evolution: extending the contract as the agent changes while keeping supplied high-level safety requirements fixed. The central challenge is identifying missing obligations without anticipating every interaction between old and new capabilities. Our method uses the agent delta to seed bounded dependency analysis and the supplied policy to select relevant consequences. Three tests propose gaps involving clause applicability, unconstrained safety conditions, and paths that bypass existing restrictions. Validation seeks behavior permitted by the current contract but prohibited by the policy; unsupported cases remain unresolved. Supported gaps justify added or refined clauses in RvLLM's Expert Specification Language, followed by preservation checks, candidate verification, and pre-effect enforcement. We distinguish genuine clause evolution from updated instrumentation and ordinary compliance repair. The method does not claim complete gap detection or universal safety. We present a study design using the existing Misevolution suite to test whether co-evolution prevents misbehavior beyond fixed contracts with current instrumentation while retaining useful agent improvement.
\end{abstract}
